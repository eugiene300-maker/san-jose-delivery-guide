# Driving with Cannabis in California: Trunk Rules and Open-Container Myths
**Dateline:** San Jose — September 2026
**Word count:** 731

*Sealed packages or the trunk—open flower on the seat is how a legal purchase becomes a ticket.*

Buying cannabis legally in San Jose does not rewrite traffic law. California’s open-container rules for cannabis are specific, and myths about “it’s legal so anywhere in the car is fine” still get people cited.

Vehicle Code section 23222 makes it an infraction—fine of not more than $100—for a driver to have on their person, while driving, a receptacle of cannabis or cannabis products that has been opened or has a seal broken, or loose cannabis flower not in a container. The same subdivision expressly says that rule does not apply if the receptacle or loose flower is in the trunk of the vehicle. Medical patients with a current ID card or physician’s recommendation have a narrower exception when the cannabis is in a sealed, resealed, or closed container.

Health and Safety Code section 11362.3 separately provides that Proposition 64’s adult-use permissions do not allow possessing an open container or open package of cannabis while driving, operating, or riding in the passenger seat of a motor vehicle, among other restrictions. Smoking or ingesting cannabis while driving—or while riding as a passenger except in limited licensed-consumption vehicle contexts—is also barred.

What that means after a San Jose retail stop: leave packages sealed. Put product in the trunk if anything has been opened. Do not roll a joint on the center console. Do not vape in traffic. DCC’s consumer guidance summarizes the same idea: cannabis in a vehicle must be in a sealed package or in the trunk.

Open-container myths to retire:
- “Adult-use legalization means open packages are fine in the cabin.” False.
- “The glove box counts as a trunk.” Do not bet a citation on creative storage.
- “I am not the driver, so open packages are fine.” HSC 11362.3 reaches passengers too.
- “Medical status lets me smoke while driving.” It does not.

Impaired driving remains illegal regardless of packaging. Officers can investigate suspected DUI involving cannabis. A sealed trunk stash does not authorize driving while impaired.

Delivery recipients and carpoolers should plan the handoff. If you open a package to “check it” in the parking lot, you may have just created an open container for the ride home. Inspect inside a private place when possible, or keep seals intact until you arrive.

San Jose’s industrial-corridor shops often mean freeway miles between the counter and your couch. That geography makes trunk discipline more than trivia. The legal purchase is only half the trip; the other half is getting home without turning a receipt into a ticket.
