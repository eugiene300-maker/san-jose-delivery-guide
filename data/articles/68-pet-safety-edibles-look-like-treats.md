*Gummies, chocolates, and baked goods confuse pets as easily as toddlers. California agencies point owners to Animal Poison Control—and to locked storage.*

San Jose’s apartment hallways and backyard-patio culture are full of dogs that can open low cabinets, counter-surf, or vacuum a dropped gummy before anyone notices. Cannabis edibles make that ordinary pet skill dangerous. Products designed to look and smell like candy or baked goods are exactly the textures many animals already beg for.

California’s Department of Cannabis Control tells pet owners to talk with a veterinarian about cannabis and animals, noting that products made for humans may include ingredients harmful to pets. CDPH’s cannabis poisoning page directs people who think a pet ingested cannabis to the Animal Poison Control Center at 1-888-426-4435. Those are not scare slogans; they are the consumer numbers to put on the fridge next to the human Poison Control line.

Symptoms owners watch for can include wobbliness, lethargy, vomiting, sensitivity to touch or sound, and unusual behavior. Do not wait to “see if it passes” if you know a pet ate an edible—or if a chewed package appears under the couch. Call Animal Poison Control or your emergency vet with the product label in hand. Be ready to report approximate weight, time of ingestion, and THC milligrams listed per serving and per package. Human chocolate, xylitol sweetener, raisins, and high-fat foods can add separate toxic problems on top of cannabinoids.

Prevention is mostly furniture and habits. Store cannabis in original child-resistant packaging inside a latched box or high cabinet pets cannot open. Do not leave purses, backpacks, or delivery bags on the floor. Remind guests—especially during 49ers watch parties or holiday hangs—that “the dog is friendly” is not the same as “the dog ignores food.” If you dose yourself with an edible, wash hands before playing tug. Reseal every time. Trash is not storage; chewed “empty” packages still attract animals.

Households with cats need the same discipline. Cats may be pickier, but curious chewing and knocked-over purses happen. Multi-pet homes should assume the most determined animal sets the security standard. If a roommate is the primary pet caregiver, make storage rules explicit in the same conversation as rent and Quiet Hours—not after an emergency clinic bill.

Legal context helps keep blame useful. Licensed California edibles carry labels and serving information that illicit brownies never will. Buying from registered San Jose retailers does not make a product pet-safe; it makes a product identifiable when a clinic asks what was eaten. That alone is a reason to avoid unlabeled gifts and parking-lot deals when animals share your square footage.

Veterinarians vary in cannabis familiarity. Some will discuss CBD products marketed for pets; many will urge caution about unregulated animal claims. Do not substitute internet dosing charts for veterinary guidance. Do not induce vomiting unless a professional specifically instructs you to. Do not assume “CBD only” packaging is harmless without reading the full label—THC contamination and terpene oils can surprise owners.

San Jose’s dense housing makes shared mistakes likelier: a delivery left on a coffee table during an unboxing video, a child-resistant jar left unlocked because “we’ll be right back,” a roommate’s stash in a closet a clever dog has opened before. Build a two-minute closing checklist when you leave the house: jars sealed, bags elevated, trash tied, gates closed.

Pets are family in South Bay culture. Treat cannabis like the age-gated medication cabinet it effectively is. Locked storage, labeled legal products, and Animal Poison Control on speed dial will not ruin your lifestyle. They will keep a normal Tuesday from becoming a frantic drive to an emergency clinic because a mango gummy looked like a training treat.

A simple household drill helps. Once a month, pretend you are a hungry dog: check floors, under couches, open backpacks, and low shelves. Anything a pet can reach fails the test. After parties, do a daylight sweep for dropped gummies before animals are let into the living room. If you foster pets or dog-sit for neighbors, disclose that cannabis is present and raise storage standards for the visit. Emergency clinic deposits are more expensive than a lockbox.
