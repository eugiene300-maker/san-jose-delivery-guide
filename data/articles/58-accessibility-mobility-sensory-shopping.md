# Accessibility: Mobility and Sensory-Friendly Shopping Tips

*Scarce storefronts and industrial addresses can be hard to navigate. A little planning makes licensed retail usable for more bodies and brains.*

San Jose’s cannabis retail map is not a downtown boutique stroll. Many registered storefronts sit in industrial corridors with uneven sidewalks, limited transit, bright lights, strong smells, and security procedures that include bag checks and multiple doors. Accessibility is a shopper-rights issue, not an afterthought.

Mobility tips: call ahead about step-free entry, elevator access, accessible parking, and whether staff can meet you at a counter height that works. Ask if delivery covers your address if traveling is the barrier—licensed delivery still requires ID and eligibility checks. Allow extra time for security vestibules. If you use a cane, walker, or wheelchair, ask where to stage belongings under the shop’s bag policy so you are not juggling product and mobility gear.

Sensory-friendly tips: visit during early quiet hours when available. Tell staff you prefer lower-stimulation help—fewer upsells, clearer one-product recommendations, written milligram notes. If fragrance or terpene intensity overwhelms you, ask about sealed pre-packs rather than open jars. Noise-canceling earbuds in line can help if store policy allows; remove them for the ID conversation. Bright menu TVs and crowded takeover weekends are optional; your calendar can choose Tuesday mornings instead.

Communication access matters. Ask whether staff can explain labels in plain language—or in Spanish if that is your stronger language—without rushing. Caregivers shopping with permission should clarify who makes the purchase decision and whose ID is required. Disability community discounts exist at some retailers; ask politely and verify requirements rather than assuming.

Accessibility does not require a featured shop tour. It requires adults to expect lawful retail to serve real bodies. If a storefront cannot meet a basic need, try another registered business or delivery rather than an informal seller who will not improve safety. The capped market is frustrating; exclusion should not be the price of entry. In San Jose’s limited storefront market, preparation beats impulse: verify the shop on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, bring ID, read the package yourself after any recommendation, and keep consumption on private property with permission. Adult-use sales require customers 21 and older. Tax totals at checkout still apply. None of those retail realities replace clinician advice when health questions are personal.
