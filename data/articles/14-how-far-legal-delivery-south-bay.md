# How Far Can Legal Cannabis Delivery Go in the South Bay?
**Dateline:** San Jose — September 2026
**Word count:** 707

*City registration and destination rules—not app radius maps—decide whether a van can lawfully stop at your door.*

South Bay shoppers often ask a radius question: will a San Jose van deliver to Campbell, Cupertino, or an unincorporated pocket near Almaden? The lawful answer is not a marketing map. It is a stack of city registration, state retail licensing, and the destination jurisdiction’s own cannabis rules.

Inside San Jose, SJPD’s Division of Cannabis Regulation requires a Notice of Completed Registration before any delivery is lawful. Ordinance 31273 aligned allowed delivery locations with allowed retail storefront locations per business, but each operator must still hold authorization. The registered businesses table’s Delivery column is the first check; calling the shop is the second. Blank cells are not a yes.

Crossing city lines adds complexity. A San Jose-registered retailer may or may not be authorized to complete sales in a neighboring city. Some South Bay cities prohibit storefronts and tightly regulate or ban commercial delivery. Unincorporated Santa Clara County prohibits commercial cannabis activity under County Code Division B26.5, which funnels many county residents into city shops for lawful retail. County code language clarifying that licensed transport on public roads to a residence outside the unincorporated area is a different issue than running a commercial drop depot in an unincorporated pocket—shoppers should not invent loopholes from transport exemptions.

State rules still apply wherever a licensed retailer operates: ID checks, daily purchase limits, child-resistant packaging, and track-and-trace inventory. DCC’s consumer guidance reminds buyers that cities and counties may be stricter than the state. A glossy app claiming “we deliver everywhere in the South Bay” is not proof of legality.

Practical checklist for distance questions:
1. Confirm the retailer on SJPD’s San Jose list if the operator claims a San Jose base.
2. Ask the shop which ZIP codes or cities it actually serves under its licenses.
3. If you live outside San Jose, check your city or county website for commercial cannabis bans or delivery rules.
4. Refuse cash-only strangers who will not name a licensed business.
5. Prefer sealed, labeled product with a receipt.

Delivery distance also intersects with driving rules. Drivers and passengers must follow open-container restrictions under Vehicle Code section 23222 and Health and Safety Code section 11362.3. Consumers receiving a delivery should keep packages sealed until they are in a private place where consumption is allowed.

San Jose’s scarce storefront count makes delivery tempting for neighborhoods far from District 3 or District 7 corridors. Temptation is not authorization. A thirty-minute drive to a registered counter remains lawful when a van cannot come. Illicit sellers exploit geography; licensed operators are constrained by it.

Bottom line: legal delivery range is a compliance question, not a GPS slider. Ask the registered retailer, verify the city list, and respect destination rules. If the van cannot come, the lawful fallback is pickup—not an unregistered account with a friendly emoji.

Adults who already prefer licensed San Jose retailers can treat the guidance above as maintenance, not conversion: verify the shop, read the label, budget for tax, and leave gray-market shortcuts alone. That steady habit is how a capped local market stays accountable to the people who shop it.

When in doubt, reopen SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses page and the Department of Cannabis Control’s consumer “what’s legal” guide before you spend cash.
