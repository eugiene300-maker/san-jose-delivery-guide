# Building a Personal Menu: Three Products, Not Thirty

*In a capped market with noisy deal calendars, a three-product personal menu beats cart chaos—and makes budtender chats faster.*

San Jose cannabis menus can feel like a streaming homepage: endless tiles, rotating brands, limited-time drops, and the low-grade anxiety that you are one visit away from missing the real thing. Shoppers who try to sample everything often learn less than shoppers who build a tight personal menu of about three products and iterate slowly.

Why three? It matches how people actually week: something for evenings, something for social weekends, something for low-key or CBD-leaning needs—or flower, edible, and a vaporizer cartridge if those are your formats. Three SKUs create comparable notes. Thirty SKUs create drawer clutter and forgotten potency surprises.

Build the menu from function, not mythology. Write one sentence each: “weekday wind-down without smoke,” “weekend movie edible at five milligrams,” “afternoon microdose that will not ruin a drive later”—then delete any sentence that implies impaired driving; if you must drive, that product does not belong in the active slot. Bring the sentences to a registered retailer and ask for two options per slot, not a tour of the entire case.

Test like a local scientist. Change one variable at a time. New edible brand, same milligrams. New flower cultivar, same evening routine. Keep a simple log: date, product, dose, onset, sleep, next-morning feel. Two weeks of notes beat a year of vibes. If a product fails twice, remove it without drama.

Deal calendars can serve the menu instead of destroying it. When a trusted brand hits a discount, restock the slot you already understand. When a stranger brand is fifty percent off, try a minimum size only if you have a free slot—or skip it. Scarcity marketing thrives on fear; your menu thrives on repetition.

Budget follows clarity. Price-per-milligram math for edibles and honest eighth pricing for flower matter more when you are not impulse-buying five side quests. Taxes will still sting in San Jose; knowing your three staples makes out-the-door totals predictable enough to plan.

Household rules shape the menu too. If roommates hate smoke, prioritize sealed formats. If pets or kids are present, prioritize locked storage and products you will finish. If workplace testing is a factor, your menu may be empty on purpose for a season—also a valid build.

Budtenders become more helpful when you arrive with a menu. “Replace my five-milligram sleep gummy; last brand felt heavy” is a solvable brief. “What’s good?” is a novel. Respect their time and your own.

A personal menu is not anti-curiosity. It is curiosity with a filing system. Rotate one slot each month if you like exploration. Keep the other two stable. In a city where storefronts are limited and promotions are loud, restraint is a consumer skill. Three products, known doses, locked storage, registered shops—that is a San Jose cannabis practice you can sustain past the next flash sale.

Local shoppers can keep this guidance beside the other essentials of adult-use life in San Jose: verify sellers on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, keep receipts, store products away from children and pets, and consume only on private property where household rules allow. Those habits will not settle every debate, but they keep ordinary weekends inside the lawful, lower-drama lane this market was built to support.
