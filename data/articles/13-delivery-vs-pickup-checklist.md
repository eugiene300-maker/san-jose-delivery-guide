# Delivery vs. Pickup in San Jose: A Verification Checklist

**Word count:** 708

*Only city-registered operators with completed registration may deliver—apps and unmarked vans do not rewrite that rule.*

Want the order at your door? In San Jose, cannabis delivery is a regulated privilege tied to city registration—not a free-for-all of apps and unmarked cars. SJPD’s FAQ is blunt: only registered cannabis businesses that applied for and received Notice of Completed Registration may conduct deliveries. Any other delivery service is unlawful under the Municipal Code.

Ordinance 31273, effective Jan. 2, 2026, amended Chapter 6.88 to align allowed delivery locations with allowed retail storefront locations per business, among other updates. That change expands what registered operators may apply to do; it does not automatically turn every storefront into a delivery hub overnight. Shoppers should still read SJPD’s registered businesses table carefully. On the May 29, 2026 snapshot, the Delivery column explicitly marks “No” for some locations. Other rows leave the cell blank. Blank is not a license to invent a “yes.” Confirm with the shop and refresh the live city list.

Verification checklist before you order:
1. Confirm the retailer appears on SJPD’s registered list.
2. Ask whether that specific location is authorized for delivery.
3. Confirm age and ID requirements—adult-use purchasers must be 21 or older; the driver will check ID.
4. Prefer payment and tracking tools the licensed retailer itself provides.
5. If a stranger offers cannabis delivery without a clear tie to a named, registered San Jose business, treat that as a red flag.

Why would a licensed shop skip delivery? Costs, security, driver staffing, inventory control, and neighborhood logistics all play roles. Downtown foot-traffic shops and industrial-corridor storefronts face different van-staging realities. Business choices do not override the city’s rule that unregistered delivery is unlawful.

Pickup remains the simpler accountability path when your preferred shop does not deliver. You see the license on the wall, the security standards, and the receipt. Delivery remains the quieter channel for people who already know what they want—provided the van belongs to a business the city actually listed.

Ordinance 31273’s broader package also shifted registration timing toward a fiscal-year calendar and reserved up to ten new equity retail or delivery registrations for equity business owners. Scarcity of new storefronts and expansion of delivery rights for those already inside the system can coexist. Speed on a phone screen is not the same as legality. A registered retailer’s delivery window may be slower than an illicit seller’s promise and still be the safer choice: tested products, known business identity, and a city complaint path if something goes wrong.

Bottom line: registration first, delivery authorization second, receipts and ID always. When in doubt, walk into a registered shop instead.

Adults who already prefer licensed San Jose retailers can treat the guidance above as maintenance, not conversion: verify the shop, read the label, budget for tax, and leave gray-market shortcuts alone. That steady habit is how a capped local market stays accountable to the people who shop it.

When in doubt, reopen SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses page and the Department of Cannabis Control’s consumer “what’s legal” guide before you spend cash. Those two bookmarks answer more first-timer questions than any parking-lot rumor.
