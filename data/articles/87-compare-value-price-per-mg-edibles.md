# How to Compare Value: Price-per-Milligram THC on Edibles

*Shelf tags lie by omission. Dividing out-the-door price by total THC milligrams turns holiday BOGOs and tiny high-potency packs into comparable numbers.*

Edible aisles in San Jose reward impulsive eyes: bright tins, brand collaborations, beverage cans, and BOGO tags that feel like victory. Value literacy is duller and more useful. For many shoppers, the cleanest comparison is price per milligram of THC after tax—not the biggest percent sticker and not the prettiest package.

Step one: read total THC in the package and THC per serving. A bag might contain ten pieces at five milligrams each (fifty milligrams total) or five pieces at ten milligrams. If you actually consume five-milligram servings, the ten-milligram pieces force breaking or accidental overshoot. Match the math to your real dose.

Step two: use the out-the-door estimate. San Jose’s combined sales and use tax rate has been listed at 10.000 percent in CDTFA guidance for recent periods, and cannabis business taxes additionally shape shelf economics. If you cannot calculate precisely in the aisle, ask for the total or add roughly ten percent to compare similarly taxed items. A “$20” bag and a “$22” bag can flip ranking after tax and discounts.

Step three: divide. Out-the-door price divided by total milligrams of THC yields cents per milligram. Example: a $28 post-tax package with 100 milligrams is $0.28 per milligram; a $18 package with 50 milligrams is $0.36 per milligram. The cheaper sticker loses. BOGO offers change the math only if you truly want two packages; a BOGO you share with a roommate can be excellent value, while a BOGO that sits until stale is waste.

Step four: adjust for format reality. Beverages may deliver faster onset than classic gummies; nano-emulsions are not mystical, but timing differs. If a drink’s convenience prevents you from redosing early, it may reduce costly overconsumption even at a higher price per milligram. Chocolates with allergens are not bargains if you cannot eat them. Sugar-free options may matter for health goals more than a tiny unit-price edge.

Step five: ignore vanity potency. High milligram packs are not “better.” DCC’s responsible-use guidance still pushes start-low patience for edibles. Buying 200 milligrams because the unit price looks pretty is how leftovers linger near kids and pets. Buy for the week you will actually supervise.

Brand premiums sometimes pay for consistency and texture; sometimes they pay for packaging art. Rotate a value brand and a premium brand in your notes. If you cannot tell a difference at your dose, stop paying for the mascot.

Do this math for two or three options before every edible run for a month. You will build intuition that survives holiday menus. Pair it with registered retailers only—illicit “edibles” without reliable milligram labels make price-per-milligram a fiction.

Value is not stinginess. It is how South Bay adults keep a taxed legal market usable without pretending every red tag is charity. Bring a notes app, divide once, and let the milligrams—not the neon—pick the tin.

Local shoppers can keep this guidance beside the other essentials of adult-use life in San Jose: verify sellers on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list, keep receipts, store products away from children and pets, and consume only on private property where household rules allow. Those habits will not settle every debate, but they keep ordinary weekends inside the lawful, lower-drama lane this market was built to support.
