*Edibles hit later and harder than many first-timers expect. California Poison Control and CDPH guidance beat internet myths when a dose goes sideways.*

Edibles are popular with San Jose shoppers who want a smoke-free option in apartments, shared houses, and HOA-sensitive neighborhoods. They are also the format most likely to produce an uncomfortable “I took too much” evening, because onset is delayed and the full effect can take hours.

California’s Department of Cannabis Control reminds consumers that edibles can take up to two hours to begin and up to four hours to peak. The folk mistake is classic: feel nothing at forty-five minutes, eat another serving, then meet both doses at once. CDPH’s cannabis poisoning materials make the same point in public-health language—edibles carry a higher poisoning risk than smoked cannabis precisely because people redose while waiting. Beverages and nano-emulsified drinks can feel faster than classic baked goods, but they still deserve the same patience and label math.

If you feel over-intoxicated—racing heart, anxiety, nausea, confusion, or a sense that time has stretched—stop consuming. Stay hydrated with water. Sit or lie down in a safe, private place. Do not drive. Do not stack alcohol or other drugs on top. Avoid “internet antidotes” that promise instant reversal. Most adult over-intoxication episodes ease with time, calm surroundings, and a trusted person nearby, but that is not a reason to tough out emergency symptoms alone. Dim lights, a familiar playlist, and a clear reminder that the feeling will pass help some people; others need quieter rooms and fewer voices. Choose what lowers panic without adding substances.

Know the escalation line. California Poison Control System advice is clear: for life-threatening symptoms such as unconsciousness, uncontrollable shaking, or difficulty breathing, call 911. For non-life-threatening but worrying symptoms, call the Poison Control hotline at 1-800-222-1222. The line is free, confidential, and staffed twenty-four hours a day, with interpreter services for many languages. Keep the product package nearby so you can report what was consumed. Do not guess milligrams if the label is still in the kitchen. Hotline staff may ask about age, weight, amount, timing, and other substances—answer accurately.

Children change the stakes. CDPH notes that kids are more sensitive because of size and weight, and may have trouble walking, sitting up, or breathing after small amounts. State Auditor materials published in 2025 cited a 469 percent increase in Poison Control calls related to cannabis ingestion among children age five and younger between 2016 and 2023. If a child may have eaten an edible, call Poison Control immediately and seek emergency care when advised. Store products in original child-resistant packaging, locked and out of sight—not in a candy dish, not in a purse on the counter, not in a backpack on a hook by the door.

Pets deserve their own plan. DCC and CDPH point pet owners to Animal Poison Control at 1-888-426-4435 if an animal may have ingested cannabis. Chocolate, xylitol, and other edible ingredients can add separate toxic risks. Keep purses and guest gifts elevated.

Prevention is the shopper skill. Start low—often 2.5 to 5 milligrams THC for new or returning consumers—and wait the full window before redosing. Read the serving size, not only the package total. Buy from registered San Jose retailers so labels reflect tested potency. Tolerance, body weight, empty stomachs, and other medications all change the experience; budtenders can discuss product categories, but they are not clinicians. If you take prescription medications, ask a clinician before making edibles a nightly habit.

Over-intoxication is usually temporary and rarely the dramatic overdose myth popular culture invents. It is still miserable enough to ruin a weekend and dangerous when someone drives, panics into unsafe behavior, or leaves products where kids can reach them. Treat edible nights like cooking with a slow oven: low heat, long wait, clear labels, and a known emergency number on the fridge—especially in a city where shared housing and curious pets are part of ordinary South Bay life.

If you are helping someone else through an edible panic, keep your own voice steady. Speak in short sentences, offer water, and discourage more cannabis “to even it out.” Do not leave them alone in a bathroom with a locked door. If symptoms worsen toward breathing trouble, collapse, or seizure-like activity, call 911 without debating internet advice. Afterward, review the label together and write down a personal serving limit for next time while the lesson is fresh.
