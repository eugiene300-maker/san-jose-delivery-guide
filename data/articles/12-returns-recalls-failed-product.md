# Returns, Recalls, and What to Do When a Product Fails You

*Licensed cannabis is not always returnable like a sweater—but recalls, complaints, and batch IDs give shoppers a path.*

Bought a vape that clogs, an edible that never hits, or flower that smells off? San Jose’s licensed market does not run on unlimited “no questions asked” returns the way some big-box retailers do. Cannabis is a controlled, trackable product. Once it leaves the counter, many shops limit returns for public-health and diversion reasons. That does not mean you are powerless when something fails.

Start with the receipt and the package label. California cannabis goods carry batch identifiers, harvest or production information, and testing-related labeling under Department of Cannabis Control rules. Keep the jar, the child-resistant packaging, and the receipt. If a retailer offers a limited quality exchange, staff will usually need those items to document the batch.

Separate personal dissatisfaction from safety. “I do not like the strain” is different from mold, a leaking cart, or a label that does not match contents. For suspected contamination or mislabeling, contact the licensed retailer first and ask about their quality process. You can also file a complaint with the Department of Cannabis Control, which accepts consumer complaints about licensed and unlicensed activity. SJPD’s buy-legal materials point residents toward city and state channels rather than anonymous street sellers when something goes wrong.

Recalls happen in regulated markets. DCC and licensed distributors issue recall notices when testing or quality problems surface. If you receive a recall notice—or see one posted about a batch you own—stop using the product and follow the retailer’s instructions for return or disposal. Do not gift recalled product to a friend. Do not try to “finish it anyway.”

What shops often cannot do: accept opened edibles for restock, take back product without ID matching the original purchase, or refund items bought from a different retailer. Policies vary; ask before you assume. Warranty-like hardware issues on vaporizers may be handled by the brand rather than the store—ask staff which path applies.

Unlicensed sellers offer the worst of both worlds: no reliable batch history and no formal recall path. If a gray-market product makes you sick, your options shrink. That asymmetry is one of the quiet arguments for SJPD-registered retailers even when menu prices feel high under Measure U and state taxes.

Practical checklist: keep receipts for at least a few weeks; photograph labels on expensive concentrates; store products as directed so heat does not create a false “defective” claim; and contact the shop promptly rather than waiting a month. If you experience a medical emergency, call 911—do not wait on a return policy.

A failed product is frustrating. In the legal system it is also documentable. Batch IDs, licensed counters, and state complaint portals are the infrastructure that turns a bad jar into a fixable incident instead of a dead end.

Adults who already prefer licensed San Jose retailers can treat the guidance above as maintenance, not conversion: verify the shop, read the label, budget for tax, and leave gray-market shortcuts alone. That steady habit is how a capped local market stays accountable to the people who shop it.

When in doubt, reopen SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses page and the Department of Cannabis Control’s consumer “what’s legal” guide before you spend cash. Those two bookmarks answer more first-timer questions than any parking-lot rumor.
