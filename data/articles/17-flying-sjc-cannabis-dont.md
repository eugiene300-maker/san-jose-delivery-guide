# Flying Out of SJC with Cannabis (Spoiler: Don’t)

*State-legal jars do not clear federal airport screening—leave cannabis out of Norman Y. Mineta San Jose International.*

San Jose shoppers sometimes treat a licensed purchase like any other souvenir. Airports do not. Norman Y. Mineta San Jose International Airport (SJC) sits in a federal screening environment. Cannabis that is legal to buy from an SJPD-registered retailer under California law remains federally controlled for most adult-use purposes, and Transportation Security Administration rules still steer travelers away from packing it.

TSA’s “What Can I Bring?” entry for medical marijuana states that marijuana is illegal under federal law and reminds travelers that TSA’s screening mission is security threats—yet if cannabis is discovered during screening, officers may refer the matter to law enforcement. TSA materials emphasize that the final decision rests with the TSA officer. Even where medical frameworks have shifted at the federal medical-scheduling level, adult-use recreational product is not a casual carry-on item, and interstate travel multiplies risk when the destination state is stricter than California.

Health and Safety Code section 11362.3 also provides that adult-use permissions do not authorize possessing an open container or open package of cannabis while riding in the passenger compartment of an aircraft. Open-package rules are the small problem; the larger problem is bringing cannabis through a federal checkpoint at all.

Practical guidance for SJC travelers:
1. Do not pack flower, vapes, edibles, or concentrates in carry-on or checked bags for commercial flights.
2. Finish, gift lawfully within California rules, or leave product at home before you head to Terminal A or B.
3. Do not assume a California medical recommendation will persuade TSA or destination police.
4. CBD product claims are a separate labeling minefield; if a product contains more than 0.3 percent delta-9 THC, treat it as cannabis for travel purposes.
5. Flying into SJC does not create a loophole for leaving with product.

Hotels near the airport and rideshare drivers cannot “hold” cannabis for you as a workaround. Consuming in airport restrooms or parking structures is still public consumption and a bad idea. If you are connecting through another state, that state’s law applies when you land—even if California sold it legally.

Why this matters for a local shopper paper: SJC is a Silicon Valley hub. Weekend visitors buy legally downtown, then try to take product home to another state. That pattern creates federal exposure and destination-state exposure that no San Jose receipt can erase. Licensed retailers can sell you tested product; they cannot federalize your suitcase.

The boring, correct plan is also the kindest to yourself: enjoy California purchases in California private spaces where rules allow, and fly clean. Buy again legally at your destination if that state has licensed retail. If it does not, do not turn SJC security into a test case.

Schedule III medical headlines may change some medical-operator accounting and research pathways over time. They do not turn adult-use eighths into airplane snacks. Until federal and airline rules clearly say otherwise for your exact product category, the South Bay traveler’s rule remains simple: don’t fly with it.
