# Hotels, Airbnb, and Consuming While Visiting San Jose

*Private property rules still govern—most hotels ban smoking, and hosts can forbid cannabis even when California allows adult use.*

Visitors who buy from SJPD-registered retailers often assume the rest of the trip is frictionless. Lodging is where California’s adult-use law meets property rights. The Department of Cannabis Control’s consumer guidance states you may use cannabis on private property, but not in public places like restaurants or bars. Property owners can ban cannabis on privately owned property. If you rent, read the rental agreement. Most hotels do not allow guests to use cannabis in the room; ask the front desk. For vacation rentals, check with the host.

That guidance maps cleanly onto a San Jose weekend. Downtown hotels near Plaza de César Chávez, Convention Center stays, and Silicon Valley business travel all come with smoke-free policies that typically include cannabis flower and often cover vaping. A balcony is not a free pass if the hotel policy bans cannabis or if smoke drifts into corridors and triggers complaints. Edibles avoid combustion smell but still leave you intoxicated in a shared building—pace doses and do not assume “no smoke” means “no rules.”

Airbnb-style rentals vary. Some hosts explicitly forbid cannabis. Others are silent, which is not the same as permission. Neighbor complaints, HOA rules, and house rules in the listing can all end a stay early. Consuming in the driveway, garage, or sidewalk outside a rental can become public consumption under Health and Safety Code section 11362.3 if the location qualifies as a public place.

Visitors should also plan the purchase timing. San Jose’s scarce storefront map means a same-day buy may require a ride to an industrial corridor. Bring a government ID showing you are 21 or older for adult-use purchases. Keep product sealed for the car ride under Vehicle Code section 23222. Do not take product to SJC for a departure flight.

Federal property remains off-limits. DCC notes cannabis is still illegal under federal law on federal lands such as national parks. That matters for day trips beyond city limits.

Practical visitor checklist:
1. Ask the hotel or host about cannabis policy before you buy.
2. Prefer edibles or sealed products if combustion is banned—and still obey intoxication and quiet-hours norms.
3. Never consume on downtown sidewalks, plazas, or park lawns.
4. Store product secured and out of sight when housekeeping or cleaners enter.
5. Leave no packaging litter; California child-resistant packing still needs responsible disposal.

San Jose tourism marketing celebrates food, sports, and downtown plazas. Cannabis hospitality exists only inside the legal retail channel and private spaces that allow it. The polite visitor treats lodging rules as seriously as ID checks at the counter. A licensed purchase is not a license to smoke out a hallway.
