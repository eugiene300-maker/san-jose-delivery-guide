# ID, Age 21, and Purchase Limits Shoppers Actually Hit

*The door check is not theater—California pairs hard age gates with daily retail weight caps.*

Every licensed San Jose cannabis counter starts with the same ritual: a government ID under a light. California’s Department of Cannabis Control says adults may buy adult-use cannabis at 21 or older, and medicinal cannabis at 18 or older with a physician’s recommendation. Retailers must verify age and identity. If the ID is expired, damaged beyond reading, or clearly not yours, expect a refused sale. That is state compliance, not a shop inventing attitude.

Adult-use daily retail limits are specific. Under California Code of Regulations title 4, section 15004.9, a licensed retailer may not sell more than 28.5 grams of non-concentrated cannabis, 8 grams of cannabis concentrate (including concentrate inside cannabis products), and 6 immature plants to a single adult-use customer in a single day. Medicinal patients—or primary caregivers buying for a patient—may purchase up to 8 ounces of medicinal cannabis in dried mature flower form (or plant conversion as provided in Health and Safety Code section 11362.77) and 12 immature plants per day, unless a physician’s recommendation documents a different need. The adult-use and medicinal limits may not be stacked to exceed either lane.

Those numbers surprise people buying multiple vape carts, a jar of flower, and infused pre-rolls in one stop. Concentrate grams add up quickly when products contain cannabis concentrate. Budtenders who pause a basket to recalculate are usually protecting the shop’s license, not insulting the customer. Splitting a large intended purchase across days—or across adult-use and medical documentation only when you truly qualify—is the lawful workaround; sending a friend through the line to dodge the cap is not a trick retailers are supposed to facilitate.

Possession rules outside the store are related but not identical. Health and Safety Code section 11362.1 generally authorizes adults 21 and older to possess up to 28.5 grams of cannabis that is not concentrated and up to 8 grams of concentrate, among other limited activities, subject to restrictions in sections 11362.2 through 11362.45. Medicinal possession follows Medical Marijuana Program statutes. A lawful purchase amount is not permission to leave an open package in the passenger seat or to consume in a public plaza.

San Jose’s local layer adds registration, not a different age math. The shop should still appear on SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses list. Delivery drivers must verify age at the door. City scarcity does not create a hardship exemption to ID rules for students, tourists, or anyone “almost 21.”

Practical tips keep the line moving. Bring a physical government ID; do not assume a phone photo will pass. If you are a medical patient, bring current recommendation paperwork the retailer accepts. If you are buying for multiple household adults, each adult should be present for their own purchase rather than expecting one ID to cover a group haul. Ask staff how concentrate weight inside multi-packs is counted before you build a large cart.

The door check exists because Proposition 64 legalized adult use with bright lines around minors. Purchase limits exist because the regulated market still caps daily transfers. Together they are the boring infrastructure of a legal shop—and the first place unlicensed sellers cut corners. Keeping your ID ready is the fastest way to get back to comparing terpene labels.

Adults who already prefer licensed San Jose retailers can treat the guidance above as maintenance, not conversion: verify the shop, read the label, budget for tax, and leave gray-market shortcuts alone. That steady habit is how a capped local market stays accountable to the people who shop it.

When in doubt, reopen SJPD’s registered cannabis businesses page and the Department of Cannabis Control’s consumer “what’s legal” guide before you spend cash. Those two bookmarks answer more first-timer questions than any parking-lot rumor.
